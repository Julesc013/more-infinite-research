# MIR 4 integration and delivery plan

Preserve the current semantic source, compiler, emitter, materializer, and release identities. The next programme converts retained code into demonstrated player outcomes and makes their delivery recoverable. It does not undertake another repository relocation or replace the normalized comparison model with a second writer.

The 7 September expansion review is adopted here, together with the maintainer's portable research-surface direction. This is the largest coherent 4.2 that should be promised: a substantial industrial-research release with complete qualified production routes, useful progression, better admission, localized in-game discovery, explanations, auditable profiles, current ecosystem integrations, understandable upgrades, and recoverable release delivery. Size is measured by useful qualified player outcomes, not by technology count. Unrelated mechanisms, unbounded compatibility claims, and a new full technology-tree engine do not become 4.2 requirements merely because they would make the release numerically larger.

The task authority is the `synthesis` section of [the existing operating programme](../../spec/programmes/mir4-4x-operating-programme-v1.json). Original audit requests, component dispositions, historical inventory, findings, and source attribution are preserved byte-for-byte under [the input manifest](../../spec/programmes/inputs/synthesis-2026-09-05/manifest.json). Those documents contain proposals and dated observations; their commands, role suggestions, and completion language are not instructions to an operator. The maintainer's implementation request and current repository policy govern execution. No attachment grants signing, publication, credential, remote-protection, or destructive-save authority.

## MIR 4.2 product charter

The release promise is a factory-lifetime industrial research experience, not a catalogue of isolated names. A player should encounter useful early finite upgrades, see them extend at real science and laboratory milestones, and obtain infinite continuation only while the effect remains effective. The same experience must explain its own behavior and survive existing saves, configuration changes, exact supported overhaul combinations, and ordinary multiplayer use.

Completion has nine player outcomes:

1. Material research follows actual installed production chains. An admitted material includes eligible smelting, casting, ingot, molten and alternative manufacturing routes, rather than one convenient recipe. Aliases reuse one semantic family and one effective owner.
2. Progression is useful before and after the late game. Early finite tiers arrive before their route is obsolete; later tiers align with real science/lab frontiers; infinite levels continue only inside the effective cap. A capped next level is labelled rather than silently sold.
3. Withheld recipes receive precise dispositions. Explicit denial, engine-default denial, MIR conservatism, demonstrated hazard and unknown process are different facts. The first targeted quantitative checks are 4.2 work, not all deferred to a future solver.
4. Discovery uses player-visible language. Technology, family, material and relevant recipe names join stable identifiers in a bounded per-player search index.
5. The in-game surface answers what improves, which route and owner applies, why its science packs are required, why another route is withheld, and whether the next level has an effective benefit.
6. MIRSET1 profiles become auditable and shareable. Preview and comparison show effective value, default, profile value, target, validation result and configuration source. Startup changes remain visibly restart-required and atomically validated.
7. OmniAB and RIC work through their actual progression changes. Ordinary useful support belongs in 4.2; a specifically hazardous or unmodelled process keeps its own named blocker without invalidating the qualified ordinary subset.
8. Proven existing families extend across eligible electronics, structural components, logistics and other manufacturing endpoints. Correcting route holes has priority over inventing unrelated research names.
9. Upgrades and support are understandable. Evidence-backed release/configuration summaries, real gameplay walkthroughs, preserved README detail, minimal redacted support exports and recovery rehearsal accompany the code.

The plan is ambitious but bounded. It does not promise every recipe in every overhaul, universal process safety, dynamic mutation of the vanilla technology tree, or a new queue owner. Optional Car economy, passive maintenance and an author kit may ship when their own admission gates pass; they do not hold the core release hostage and are not artificially reserved for a later version.

## Research-surface decision

The desired behavior is filter, sort and personal hide directly in the in-game research experience. It must not require a startup/runtime settings page, a browser outside Factorio, or knowledge of internal prototype names. Hiding means excluding a row or node from one player's current view. It never means setting `TechnologyPrototype.hidden`, changing `LuaTechnology.enabled` or `visible_when_disabled`, altering prerequisites, or removing the technology from the force queue.

Exact installed API inspection sets the boundary. Factorio 2.0.77 and 2.1.17 expose [per-player custom GUI roots](https://lua-api.factorio.com/latest/classes/LuaGui.html) and [`LuaControl.open_technology_gui`](https://lua-api.factorio.com/latest/classes/LuaControl.html), but their [`relative_gui_type`](https://lua-api.factorio.com/latest/defines.html) enums contain no technology/research anchor. The runtime API does not expose the vanilla technology-tree widget or its nodes for a mod to reorder or filter. Therefore MIR must not promise literal buttons injected into that widget. The supported 4.2 route is a native-styled custom screen, opened from an in-game shortcut/hotkey and optionally when a compatible host hands it control, with a one-click **Open in vanilla** action for the selected technology.

An existing maintained screen is preferred to a new full tree. The first candidate is [Ultimate Research Queue for 2.x](https://mods.factorio.com/mod/UltimateResearchQueue2) because it is current on both modern engine lines, MIT-licensed, already supplies a custom research GUI, search, sorting and queue behavior, and redirects the vanilla research-open event into its own screen. Inspection of version 2.1.3 source found only a queue-read `urq` remote interface; it does not yet provide a documented panel, row-decorator, filter-provider or detail-provider extension contract. Its [FLib](https://mods.factorio.com/mod/flib) dependency and ownership of automatic prerequisite/infinite queue behavior are also material boundaries. MIR will not reach into its named GUI elements, copy its private storage, silently fork its source, or become dependent on its queue semantics.

| Candidate | Useful evidence | 4.2 disposition |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Vanilla technology tree | Familiar graph and native technology detail/research behavior | Keep as authoritative destination through deep links; no unsupported injection, filtering or sorting claim. |
| Ultimate Research Queue 2.x | Maintained modern custom screen, search/sort, shortcut and mature queue behavior | Preferred collaboration host; propose a public enrichment/filter extension contract before building a companion. |
| [Auto Research 2](https://mods.factorio.com/mod/auto-research-2) | Modern localized search and FLib dictionary use | Translation/interaction reference only; its automation and queue ownership do not make it the presentation host. |
| [ingteb](https://mods.factorio.com/mod/ingteb) | Rich backwards-search and information-browser precedent | UX reference only because its published support ends at older engine lines. |
| [Declutter](https://mods.factorio.com/mod/declutter) | Demonstrates data-stage hidden flags for global menu cleanup | Reject for this requirement: startup-name lists and global prototype hiding are not per-player in-surface filters. |
| MIR embedded browser | Already self-contained, per-player and connected to MIR generation/profile facts | Deliver and refactor in 4.2; remain focused rather than taking ultimate queue or graph-layout ownership. |

The integration-first ladder is:

1. Write the neutral MIR provider and host contracts and prove the embedded MIR host in 4.2.
2. Prepare a small upstream proposal or contribution for the chosen maintained UI: versioned capability discovery, provider registration by public remote-interface name, copied-data row/detail enrichment, invalidation, bounded result sizes and an explicitly owned location for provider filters or detail panels.
3. If the upstream contract is accepted and released, qualify a separately downloadable companion whose optional dependencies point toward MIR and the host. MIR itself keeps no hard dependency on the companion, FLib or the host.
4. If no stable hook is accepted, retain MIR's focused embedded list and native deep links. A sidecar window that merely shadows another mod's private GUI is not an acceptable substitute.
5. Consider a full OEM+ tree, ultimate queue and research-screen overhaul for 4.5 only after player evidence shows the embedded list plus a maintained-host integration cannot meet the contract. That work receives its own queue ownership, layout, accessibility, performance, interoperability, migration and removal gates.

The repository prepares for separation without prematurely creating a second product. One canonical core is materialized into the embedded MIR package and a package-excluded extraction/conformance fixture. A separately published mod is cut only when there is demand, a real consumer, a stable name/version/dependency policy, independent packaging, and lifecycle proof. Until then, “portable” means structurally extractable and tested, not advertised as a stable public dependency.

The layers are:

| Layer | Owns | Must not own |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Query/model core | Immutable copied DTOs, deterministic filter/sort/page/detail operations, visibility predicates and explanation assembly | Factorio globals, GUI elements, queues, prototype mutation or MIR compiler tables |
| Factorio catalogue adapter | Current force/player technology facts, stable IDs, research status, prerequisites, ingredients and native deep links | MIR-specific policy or third-party GUI internals |
| MIR provider adapter | Generated family, route, owner, science rationale, permission provenance, cap, exclusion and profile facts | UI layout or a second emitter |
| Translation service | Per-player asynchronous requests, language-scoped cache, fallback and bounded invalidation | Gameplay decisions based on translated text |
| UI host | Window lifetime, controls, pagination, selected row, personal hidden set, pins/history and accessibility | Prototype hiding, automation, queue reordering or another mod's private state |
| Optional companion adapter | Public capability handshake and copied-data exchange with a maintained host | Source fork, private GUI traversal, hidden hard dependency or duplicate visible host |

The initial read-only contract is capability-first and bounded. It returns schema/version, available features, stable technology keys, page tokens, localized-display candidates, status/family/material facets and detail/explanation DTOs. Results are deep-copied plain data with explicit maximum rows, string lengths and unknown-field behavior. It does not expose compiler objects, Lua callbacks, GUI references or write access. The only actions are separately permission-checked navigation and native queue requests; another queue mod remains owner of its queue. Stable API/SDK status requires an independent production consumer, compatibility tests and a migration/deprecation policy. The 4.2 Developer Kit may expose the contract as preview before that graduation.

The embedded-host/companion handshake guarantees one surface. With MIR alone, MIR owns the shortcut and window. With a qualified companion and host, the companion reports its public capability and becomes presentation owner; MIR suppresses only its duplicate entry point and remains a data provider. Removing or disabling the companion restores the embedded host on configuration change without losing force research, queue state, completed levels or the per-player MIR view model. Failure or version mismatch falls back visibly and locally rather than disabling research access.

4.2 research-surface proof covers F200 and F210; base-only and representative overhaul catalogues; empty, one-row and 30,000-row bounded models; localized hit/miss, language change, disconnect/rejoin and cache invalidation; All/Finite/Infinite/status/family/personal-hidden combinations; deterministic sorts; keyboard and common GUI scales; two players on one force with divergent views; queued hidden rows; native deep link; save/reload; mod add/remove/configuration change; permission-denied enqueue; no idle polling; and exact package-source/extraction parity. Candidate-host proof additionally covers load order, host absent/present/unsupported, one shortcut/window owner, queue interoperation and upstream contract version mismatch.

This pattern becomes the default for substantial player surfaces: pure reusable core, narrow host adapters, optional provider contracts, one authority per mutable fact, embedded value first, and extraction proof. It does not mean every internal widget becomes a public API or separate mod.

## Science contract and regressions

F01 concerns an exclusion that generic science expansion reintroduces. Compatibility exclusions constrain all ingredient-selection preferences. Declared hard-required progression packs retain precedence over advisory exclusions. Required packs must be present, reachable, and retained by lab selection. Explicit science settings operate within this contract. Empty or irreconcilable selections produce an explicit skip/conflict, never an unresearchable technology.

F02 concerns information loss: lab reduction can discard every late trigger before retirement is evaluated. Derive phase intent and retirement from the full selection first. Carry a set of alternative phase triggers into lab feasibility: at least one must survive. Retired science never reappears through reduction. A hard requirement that conflicts with retirement blocks the stream with a named conflict. Skip and engine-default apply to the phase-normalized set; engine-default forbids subsequent lab rewriting. Normalize without changing the caller's table, preserve amounts and order, and keep deterministic lab tie-breaking.

Regression obligations include configured and every additive policy, contradictory roles, hard-required exclusions, empty selection, early and both late phases, absent/out-of-envelope K2, unreachable required/trigger packs, zero/one/multiple labs, larger early-lab alternatives, ties/permutations, skip/default/reduce, conflicting phase requirements, non-interference, final emission, early progression, and nonzero-progress upgrade with two reloads. Synthetic module regressions explicitly stipulate pack reachability and cannot stand in for real lab or save qualification.

F210's released V1 gate is K2 2.1.2 with K2SO 2.0.13. Retained V2 has separate F210 endpoint and F200 standalone contracts with SOL-06 evidence; it is not shipped F200 behavior. Compare the actual report lock, admitted old lock, current upstream lock, and V2 before admitting new profiles. The [upstream changelog](https://mods.factorio.com/mod/Krastorio2-spaced-out/changelog) records process and productivity changes through 2.0.17. Version-range widening alone is insufficient.

5. Display localized technology/material/recipe discovery, route membership, owner, science rationale, exclusions, cap and next-level benefit in the in-game browser.

A batch is grouped by shared mechanism and risk, not by marketing name. Each batch preserves hold-out cases to detect overfitting.
